Return to the Geometric Neuron

Six copies of the accidental 2025 loop, wired to each other. Nothing here is trained. The wiring and two small rules are written by hand, and the only thing that changes with experience is a number that was already in the file: every edge in ecg.json carries learned_weight: 1.0, and nothing ever wrote to it.

The finding in one line: a unit can only be taught by a beat it hears after it has forgotten its own. Use that as the writing rule and what the colony learned survives a seizure of any length, as long as the seizure is faster than the units can forget.

One unit beats when it forgets its last beat

That was this morning's result in GeometricNeuronOriginReview. The controller keeps the last 50 readings. A beat leaves two huge readings in that memory, and while they sit there the loop is held quiet. When they fall out, it kicks, goes blind, and beats again: 59 ticks. It has a threshold, a refractory period and a reset, and nothing in it changes slowly. One attractor, no memory.

Where you listen on the surface decides what you hear

The splitter has 16 outputs. They are the top row of the checkerboard picture, and the loop only ever used the first four, the corner. From the corner, a unit is blind to its own kick just before the beat: it reads zero. Four outputs further along the same row, the same moment reads 3.75 to 4.0, as loud as the beat itself. So a neighbour listening mid-row hears the beat coming four ticks before the unit that is about to make it. This is the one place where the original idea, geometry on the surface, does real work.

What one edge does, as its weight grows (16 random starts each):

WeightListening mid-row (outputs 6–9)Listening at the corner (outputs 0–3)

Two things follow. A pair has several stable relationships where a single unit had one attractor, so relationships can hold something. And a strong edge here is not excitation: a heard beat counts as the listener's own, so it makes the listener refractory. Too much listening is silence.

Experience, seizure, release

Four of the six units are made to beat in order, 0→1→2→3 or 3→2→1→0, for 4,000 ticks. Then the colony is left alone. Then every unit is forced to beat together every 20 ticks: the seizure. Then it is left alone again and I ask what is left of the order. Three colonies, 12 random starts for each order.

The news rule writes about 5 times during the whole seizure, all on its first beat. After that every unit remembers a beat at every moment, so nothing it hears is news. The refractory window that makes the pulse also write-protects the unit.

The loss from +0.20 to +0.13 does not happen in the seizure. It happens in the recovery, when the units come out in step, forget together, and write each other while they drift apart. Without a seizure the same stretch of ordinary life costs less (+0.20 → +0.19).

Second round: what the loop already does with timing

Sol (ChatGPT) read the code and made two fair points. The frozen colony I compared against had identical units, so its staying in step proved little. And before adding anything, measure whether one heard pulse already has a different effect depending on when it lands. I ran both.

One pulse, 59 arrival times. A single two-tick pulse reaches a lone unit at each phase of its cycle. The dots show how many ticks later its next beat comes.

Three regimes, with nothing trained. A weak pulse is not heard at any phase. A strong one is heard at every phase but the last three, and the delay equals the phase: the unit restarts its clock from the heard beat. In between, at weight 0.2, the same pulse gets through only on three ticks out of every eight, and then costs almost a whole beat (55 ticks). So the loop has a fast gate inside its slow cycle. The eight is twice the loop delay; I take it to be the idle ripple but did not test that by changing the delay. A pulse never makes the beat come earlier.

The missing control. I took each learned set of weights, shuffled which unit is which, froze it, and put it through the same seizure, next to the learned set frozen as it was.

Sol was right about the first row: falling out of step needs only unequal weights, not learning. What learning adds is the next three rows. The order comes back in the beating itself after the seizure, nearly at full strength (+0.29 / −0.25 before), and influence has a direction: a taught leader moves its follower nearly three times as far as the follower moves it. With shuffled weights there is no direction.

This also corrects my first ledger. The weak order in the beats after a seizure (+0.14 / −0.10) was not the memory failing to express itself. It was the colony rewriting itself during recovery. Hold the weights still and the behaviour returns.

Third round: two orders, one cue

The test Sol proposed: teach both orders into one colony, freeze the weights, make unit 0 or unit 3 beat once, and see whether the colony follows the cued order. It did not. The numbers are the fraction of 96 trials in which units 0–3 beat in exactly the cued order within 40 ticks.

ColonyTaught one order, matching cueSame, no cue at allTaught both orders
As it was: a heard beat only quiets0.090.100.00 – 0.01
Plus a trigger: a heard beat can make the listener beat0.060.090.00, and a runaway: 378 beats where 14 is normal
Plus a trigger and a 10-tick refractory0.510.380.07 / 0.01

With a trigger and a short refractory, one stored order does replay from its head about half the time, and never backwards. But it also replays unprompted in over a third of windows, because any natural beat of the head unit starts it. Two orders in one colony could not be separated at any threshold I tried. The weights show why: taught one order, the edge to the next unit in line (0.14–0.23) stands clear of the edge two away (0.04–0.11). Taught both, every edge is 0.04–0.15 and the two overlap. Pairwise weights hold who beat near whom. They have no place to hold which order is meant.

The trigger and the refractory are my additions, not part of ecg.json, and were switched on only for this test.

Ledger

Shown

  • The browser colony and the Python colony agree to six decimals over 5,000 ticks.
  • Order of experience is written into the weights by the news rule (+0.20 against −0.20 for the reverse order) and read back from the weights after a seizure in 22 of 24 runs.
  • Weight change during a fast seizure is 0.0001 whether it lasts 1,000 or 16,000 ticks.
  • The protection ends where predicted, at the forgetting time.
  • A colony of identical units is locked in step for good by a single common beat. Unequal weights are enough to break that; learning is not needed for it.
  • With weights frozen after learning, the order returns in the beating after a seizure (22 of 24 runs; shuffled weights 14 of 24), and a leader moves its follower three times as far as the reverse.
  • The untrained loop gates a heard pulse by its arrival time, on the 59-tick cycle and on an 8-tick ripple inside it.

Not shown

  • That the memory is useful. It orders beats and directs influence; it has not been set against any task or any simple sequence-memory baseline.
  • Selection. Tested and not achieved: two orders in one colony could not be told apart by a cue.
  • That it lasts. Ordinary life keeps writing, and the order fades with no seizure at all.
  • Unlearning. No rule here makes a weight smaller for a reason.
  • Anything beyond six units, 12 starts per order, one task, one hebb rule as comparison.
  • That the protection is a discovery. The gate was designed; what was measured is that this loop's own variance really does hold it shut, and exactly where it stops.
